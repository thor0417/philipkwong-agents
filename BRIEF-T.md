# BRIEF T. ONE STANDARD, EVERY MARKET.

Write this file to the repo before starting.

## Why this before the rest of S

Brief Q found that fifteen projects could carry a referral brief today and all
fifteen are Clark County. Not because Clark has better projects. Because Clark
is the only jurisdiction with a conditions reader, and no other market's
projects fail on parties or facts. They fail on conditions alone.

That was never a decision. Clark County was mapped first and thoroughly, and
every market after it got as far as its records and stopped. Nothing defines
what "covered" means, so a market gets added when its records arrive rather than
when it reaches a standard.

Delivering a one-market product weekly is premature. So this runs before Brief S
items 2 through 5. S item 1 is already built.

## Standing rules

Measure before you build. A rule ships with the count behind it and the cost
per market stated. Report before you propose. Ask what a source publishes as a
FIELD before opening a PDF. Read the body, not the status code. Never
fabricate. Nothing is silently absent. Sweep for the shape before the fix is
committed. State the cap beside any figure taken from a capped read. NPM_EXIT
from the captured line on every gate.

## ITEM 1. DEFINE THE STANDARD FROM WHAT CLARK COUNTY ACTUALLY DOES

Do not invent it. Read Heart Hotel and Tropicana Land and report what a Clark
County project carries that the others do not, field by field, with the reader
and the document that produces each.

My reading, to be confirmed or corrected by the measurement:

  - a named private party, with a firm where the record states one
  - stated facts from the filing: acreage, zone, storeys, rooms, parking
  - conditions of approval, numbered and quoted
  - a decision, with the body and the date

Report which of those four Clark produces, from which document, through which
reader, and what proportion of Clark projects carry each.

Then state the standard as a testable definition.

---

## ITEM 2. MEASURE EVERY MARKET AGAINST IT

For each market we hold, report per criterion: how many live projects carry it,
and where it comes from when it does.

Then, for every criterion a market fails, answer the question nobody has asked:
**which document would carry it, and do we fetch that document at all?**

That is the point of this brief. A market failing on conditions because the
county publishes none is a different answer from a market failing because we
never open the staff report.

For each market and each missing criterion, report one of:

  - the document exists and we fetch it, and the reader does not parse it
  - the document exists and we do not fetch it, with its URL shape
  - the document exists behind something we cannot reach, named
  - the jurisdiction does not publish it, with what you checked

Anaheim, Phoenix, Nashville and Oakland have never been probed for a conditions
document at all. New York's CPC report was measured and mostly does not carry
them, and the open case names two candidates nobody has looked at: the Council
approval resolution and the ACRIS restrictive declaration.

---

## ITEM 3. RANK WHAT IT WOULD COST TO BRING EACH MARKET TO STANDARD

For every market below standard, report the work in order of cost, cheapest
first: a config entry, a reader change, a new reader, a new source, or not
possible.

State how many projects each would lift to standard, measured, not estimated.

I want to know which single change lifts the most projects, and whether any
market is out of reach entirely.

---

## ITEM 4. MAKE IT A CHECK

A market is either at standard or explicitly below it, and nothing drifts
silently again.

Build `verify:market-standard` beside `verify:coverage-table`. Per market, per
criterion, with the count. A market below standard does not fail the check; it
is reported with what it is missing, so the next market added is measured on
arrival rather than three weeks later.

Then report what a client document should say about a market below standard.
My position: the coverage note names it. A Nashville project reading thin
without the document saying the market cannot go deeper is the failure the whole
provenance layer exists to prevent.

Then Brief S items 2 through 5.
